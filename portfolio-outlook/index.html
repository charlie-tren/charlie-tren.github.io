What 8,000 shares went on to do since 1962, turned into expected returns for 7,800 shares, ETFs and Bitcoin funds. In Australian dollars, updated weekly.

    Mean Return—

    Each holding's mean return, weighted by its share of the portfolio.

    Volatility—likely swing in a year

    From each holding's price swings over three years and how closely the holdings move together, crashes included.

    Sharpe Ratio—

    Mean return above cash, divided by volatility.

    P(Gain)—

    Chance the whole portfolio ends higher, from its mean return and volatility.

    Bad Year—1 year in 20 is this bad

    A one-year result this bad or worse comes about one year in twenty.

    Correlation—

    How closely the holdings have moved together, from 0 (unrelated) to 1 (in step). Shares: three years of weekly prices, moved halfway to how they moved in crashes.

    Allocation

        Method

        What This Estimates

        A best guess at what a holding will return over the next one, three or five years, including dividends, in Australian dollars. It comes from what similar holdings returned in the past.

        Worked Example

        Starting Point

        About 7,600 shares listed today in the US, Japan, Australia, Canada and the UK, plus 454 US companies that later delisted, with prices back to 1962. On average they rose about 4% a year before dividends.

        Adjustments

        Shares close to their 52-week high (the highest price of the past year) have tended to beat shares far below it. So have companies listed for many years, and US companies buying back their own shares.

        The best fifth of shares on these measures beat the worst fifth by 6-9 percentage points a year. In Japan none of them held, and Japanese shares keep the starting point.

        These hold for the next year. Over three and five years only US buybacks still helped, at three years, so the longer estimates stay close to the starting point.

        Left Out

        About 320 other measures were tested, including momentum, price to earnings, profitability, analyst price targets and earnings surprises. Each was tried on earlier years, then checked against the year that followed, for every year from 1995 to 2025.

        None improved the estimate. Giving all of them a small weight made it worse in 61% of years.

        Delisted Companies

        Share price records usually drop companies that stop trading. In the US, shares still listed today returned about 5% a year more than all shares did. Each estimate takes this off: up to 14% a year for small, volatile companies and almost nothing for the largest. In Japan most delistings were takeovers, and the deduction is close to zero.

        Dividends

        Dividends count in full up to 6% a year and for less above that. Shares yielding 6-10% did no better than average the following year, and those yielding over 10% did worse.

        Track Record

        What the holding did from its own past times this far below its high counts for at most 16% of the estimate, and less when there have been only one or two.

        ETFs, Bitcoin and Cash

        Index ETFs get the average return of 58 country, sector and style ETFs, including Greece and Turkey. Bitcoin funds use the Bitcoin Power Law Forecast, trimmed where its past forecasts ran high, and a 2x fund has trailed twice the plain fund by about 15% a year. Cash ETFs use their own record.

        Currency

        No exchange-rate move is assumed. Nothing tested predicted exchange rates better than assuming no change. Past currency moves widen the range for overseas holdings.

        Reading the Figures

        The median return is what one holding usually returns. The mean return is the average result, and a few big winners lift it above the median. Means are then cut to what past forecasts like them actually returned, by a third to a half for the most-traded shares. The portfolio total uses the mean.

        Middle 50% is the range half of similar holdings ended in, and P(Gain) is how often they finished higher. Three and five year figures are per year.

        Limits

        For any single share the estimate is close to a coin flip. A list of the 20 highest-ranked shares did not beat owning everything. Taxes and trading costs are not included.

        Highest Median Returns

        Shares only, ranked by median return over the horizon chosen above. A list of the highest-ranked shares did not beat owning the whole market in testing, so treat it as a place to start looking, not a buy list.
