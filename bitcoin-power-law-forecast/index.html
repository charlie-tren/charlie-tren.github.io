Bitcoin's price compared with its long-run power-law trend, and a forecast of where it could be in one to four years. Updated daily.

The forecast blends two models. The first expects price to move back toward the trend, as it did after the 2015, 2018 and 2022 lows. The second expects each four-year cycle to grow less than the one before.

Models

Power-Law Trend

A straight line fitted through Bitcoin's daily prices since July 2010, with both price and time (days since 3 January 2009) on log scales.

Reversion Model

Price grows with the trend, and part of the gap to the trend closes. Up to six months ahead it also uses momentum: price against its 200-day average. Since 2021 its 1-2 year forecasts have come in 8-16% too high.

log10(P[t+h] / P[t]) = log10(T[t+h] / T[t]) + a + b log10(P[t] / T[t]) + c log10(P[t] / M[t])

Slowing-Growth Model

The four-year average price keeps growing, at a rate that has been falling since 2015, and part of the gap between price and that average closes.

log10(P[t+h] / P[t]) = g(A[t], h) + a + b log10(P[t] / A[t])
