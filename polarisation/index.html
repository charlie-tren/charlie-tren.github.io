What the Parties Disagree About

How far apart the parties stood at each election, weighted by the votes they got, against where that same measure sat in the 1970s.

Every party standing at an election carries a position on each axis, coded by V-Dem's country experts. The spread is the standard deviation of those positions across the parties, weighted by the share of the vote each one actually received, so a party nobody votes for barely moves it.

An election counts only where the parties carrying a coding hold at least 70% of the vote cast. That floor exists because joint lists, such as Hungary's Fidesz-KDNP, put the vote on a row with no position of its own, and scoring the election on whoever was left would quietly measure a different country.

The line is the median across countries, divided by that same median in the 1970s. So 1.0 is exactly where that axis stood fifty years ago, and the chart is about change rather than level: the axes are on different scales and their raw spreads were never comparable.
