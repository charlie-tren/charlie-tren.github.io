Gold's price and where it could be in one to five years. Updated monthly.

Three things drive the forecast: gold's average growth since 1968, its move over the last 12 months, and its price against the US money supply.

Method

Long-Run Growth

Gold's average monthly change since March 1968, when the London Gold Pool ended.

log(P[t+h] / P[t]) = g h

Momentum Tilt

Gold has tended to keep moving for 3-12 months the way it moved over the last 12. That move carries into the first year of the forecast.

log(P[t+h] / P[t]) = a + b log(P[t-1] / P[t-13])

Valuation Model

Gold's price against the US money supply, allowing 1.7% a year for new mining. When gold is expensive against money, it expects lower returns. Its weight rises from zero at one year to at four and at five, set by how each model's forecasts since 1985 performed.

log(P[t+h] / P[t]) = a + b (log(P[t-1] / M[t-1]) + 0.017 y[t-1])

Ranges

How far past forecasts landed from the actual price, for forecasts made every month since 1985 with only data known then.

Chance of a 20% Fall

Share of 2,000 simulated paths that drop 20% below today's price before the horizon. Each path strings together six-month runs of gold's actual monthly moves since 1968, centred on the median forecast.

Tested and Rejected

About 2,850 other ideas, alone and combined: power laws, trend curves, cycles, interest rates, the dollar, inflation, central bank buying, jewellery spending and other metals. None beat the long-run average in forecasts made with only data known at the time.
